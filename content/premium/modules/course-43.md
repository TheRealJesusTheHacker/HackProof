Jobsite Safety, First Aid & Licensing Pathways
MODULE OVERVIEW
Level: Beginner | Time: 60 minutes | Prerequisites: None. Required before a new field hire shadows install work.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
After this module you can:
- Apply a safety mindset to ladders, lifts, and PPE
- Explain how the OSHA 10-Hour card is earned
- Describe the First Aid/CPR/AED in-person skills pathway
- Explain why low-voltage licensing varies by place
- List Sentinel's baseline expectations for field roles

LESSON 1 — JOBSITE SAFETY MINDSET
Safety is a habit, not a form. Walk the area first: trip hazards, overhead work, wet floors, open panels. If conditions change, stop and reassess. You always have authority to pause an unsafe task and raise it with your lead.

Ladders: use the right ladder, inspect it, set it level, keep three points of contact, never stand on the top cap or overreach - climb down and move it. Lifts: only trained, authorized operators use them, per manufacturer instructions and site rules. If you are not trained on that lift, do not operate it.

Electrical awareness: treat every conductor as live until a qualified person verifies otherwise. Never open line-voltage panels outside your training and authorization. Report damaged cords instead of working around them.

PPE checklist:
- Eye protection when drilling, cutting, or working overhead
- Gloves for the task; hearing protection around loud equipment
- Hard hat where required; report injuries and near misses same day

CASE BREAKDOWN: An installer leans a ladder at a shallow angle rather than fetching the taller one. It slips as he drills; he is unhurt, but the drill damages the client's wall. The near miss was reported and ladder setup re-briefed that day.

COMMON MISTAKE: Treating PPE as optional on small jobs. Most injuries happen on routine tasks. The short job gets the same discipline.

LESSON 2 — OSHA 10, FIRST AID/CPR/AED, AND LICENSING
The OSHA 10-Hour card comes from OSHA's Outreach Training Program, earned by completing a 10-hour construction or general industry course from an OSHA-authorized trainer or authorized online provider. It is orientation training - not a license and not a substitute for task training.

First Aid/CPR/AED is earned through the American Heart Association or American Red Cross, with skills demonstrated in person - compressions and AED use must be performed, not just read about. Cards expire and are renewed through those organizations.

Low-voltage licensing varies: some states license it, some cities require local permits, alarm work can have separate rules. Pathway: identify the authority where the work is performed, confirm current requirements, complete its application, background check, and training steps. Never assume one place's license covers another.

Field roles also require a valid driver's license and a clean background check.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A new hire presents an instant online CPR card with no skills session. Sentinel does not accept it. The hire is scheduled into an AHA or Red Cross course before unsupervised work.

COMMON MISTAKE: Assuming a credential counts everywhere. It is valid only as its issuing body defines it, and only where that authority applies.

HANDS-ON EXERCISE
Build a plan for Alex, a new hire with no OSHA card, an expired CPR card, and a valid driver's license, working in two cities.
Decision point 1: A site requires an OSHA 10 card next month. Do you buy the fastest card from any seller? Correct path: No. Enroll only with an OSHA-authorized trainer or provider.
Decision point 2: Alex offers the expired card plus this module as first aid proof. Correct path: Not sufficient. Schedule an AHA or Red Cross in-person skills course first.
Decision point 3: Alex is licensed in the first city. Correct path: Do not assume coverage. Confirm requirements with the second city's authority first.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Pause any unsafe task and raise it with your lead
- Lifts and line-voltage work are for trained, authorized people only
- OSHA 10 is earned through authorized Outreach Training
- First Aid/CPR/AED requires in-person AHA or Red Cross skills
- Licensing varies - verify with the local authority

GLOSSARY
PPE - Personal protective equipment, such as eye protection
OSHA Outreach Training Program - Program through which authorized trainers deliver 10-hour courses
Near miss - An incident that could have caused injury but did not
Low-voltage - Data, camera, and alarm cabling, distinct from line-voltage power
AED - Automated external defibrillator
Issuing authority - Body that grants a license, permit, or card

CERTIFICATION ALIGNMENT
Pathway orientation for: the OSHA 10-Hour card (issued through OSHA's Outreach Training Program via authorized trainers), First Aid/CPR/AED (issued by the American Heart Association or American Red Cross, including in-person skills), and state or local low-voltage licensing (issued where the work is performed). This module explains the pathway and teaches supporting knowledge; the credential itself is earned through the issuing body, including any hands-on, in-person, or state requirements. No online course can grant any of these credentials.

MODULE OVERVIEW
Level: Foundations | Time: ~20 minutes
Prerequisites: none

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
After this module you can:
- Explain why a familiar voice no longer proves identity
- Recognize the jail, accident, and kidnapping versions of this scam
- Use a family code word and call-back rule to stop a fake emergency
- Set up a family plan before a scammer tests your household

LESSON 1 — WHY THE VOICE PROVES NOTHING NOW
A voice clone is made from a short sample of real speech. A public video, voicemail greeting, or social media clip can give a scammer enough to copy tone, accent, and common phrases. In a panicked call, your brain hears your child or grandchild and fills in the rest. That reaction is normal, and it is what the scammer counts on.

The script is consistent. Someone in trouble needs money fast, begs you not to tell anyone, and pushes you to pay before you can think. A second voice often joins as a lawyer, officer, or doctor to sound official and keep you on the line while you drive to the bank, so you cannot call the real person.

CASE BREAKDOWN: Carol gets a call at 9:40 p.m. A young man sobbing says, Grandma, I was in a car accident, I am at the police station. A man then says he is an attorney and $8,500 in bail must be sent by wire or gift cards tonight, and warns her not to call the parents. Carol recognizes the voice and panics. Where it could have stopped: at the secrecy demand and the payment method. Bail is not paid in gift cards, and real emergencies do not require hiding the call from family. The signal most people miss: the caller creates a closed loop, supplying the story, deadline, and payment steps while blocking every step that would let you check.

What to do: Say, I am going to hang up and call you back on your number. Do not debate whether it sounds real. Hang up and call the person on a number you already have. If there is no answer, call another family member. Never use a number the caller gave you.

COMMON MISTAKE: Accepting yes, or a childhood nickname, as proof. Scammers learn nicknames online, and a clone can say any scripted words.

LESSON 2 — THE CODE WORD AND CALL-BACK DEFENSE
Your defense cannot be your ear. It must be a procedure followed even when scared.

First, choose a family code word: a word or phrase that is easy to remember, not posted online, not a pet name, and not used as a password. Every adult and older child learns it. In an emergency you ask, What is our code word. A real family member knows it. A clone operator should not. Change it if it is shared outside the family.

Second, adopt a non-negotiable call-back rule. Any call asking for money, secrecy, or immediate action gets ended, no matter how real it sounds. You call back on a saved number. For a hospital, jail, or police station, look up the facility yourself and call its published number.

Say this, word for word if needed: I love you. Our family rule is I have to hang up and call you back, and I need our code word. I am hanging up now. Then hang up, even mid-sentence. Do not send money while emotional and on the original call. If someone may truly be in danger, call local police or 911 and let them help you verify.

Share the plan in advance and rehearse it once so asking feels normal, not rude. Scammers target the relative most likely to pay quickly and tell no one.

COMMON MISTAKE: Creating a code word but never practicing it. Under stress, people forget new rules.

HANDS-ON EXERCISE
A cloned voice that sounds like your son says he was arrested after an accident, and a man claiming to be his lawyer needs a wire transfer within the hour. The lawyer says do not call your son's phone because it was confiscated.
Decision point 1: Stay on the line, or hang up. Correct path: Hang up. Staying gives the scammer control and blocks verification.
Decision point 2: The caller rings back, crying harder. Do you resume. Correct path: No. Call your son on his saved number, then another family member. Urgent callbacks are pressure, not proof.
Decision point 3: You cannot reach your son. Do you pay a smaller amount just in case. Correct path: No. Pay nothing until identity is verified independently, through family, police, or the facility on a number you find yourself.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- A familiar voice is no longer evidence of identity
- Jail, accident, and kidnapping stories all use urgency and secrecy
- Ask for the code word, then hang up and call back on a saved number
- Never pay by wire or gift card on an unverified emergency call
- Set and rehearse the plan with relatives before a call comes

GLOSSARY
Voice clone — An artificial copy of a person's voice made from real speech samples
Code word — A private phrase family members use to prove a real emergency call
Call-back rule — Ending a suspicious call and redialing a trusted saved number
Social engineering — Manipulating emotion or trust to get money or information
Closed loop — A setup where the caller controls the story, contacts, and payment steps
Bail scam — A fake arrest story used to demand urgent payment for release

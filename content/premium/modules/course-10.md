MODULE OVERVIEW
Level: Intermediate | Time: ~20 minutes
Prerequisites: none

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
After this module you can:
- Define vishing and explain how spoofed caller ID defeats your first instinct
- Recognize bank fraud department, agency, and tech support call scripts
- Apply the hang-up-and-call-back rule using the number on your card
- Refuse money movement, codes, and remote access requests on any incoming call

LESSON 1 — THE VOICE YOU CANNOT TRUST ON ITS OWN
Vishing is voice phishing: fraud over a phone call. Its central tool is spoofed caller ID. A scammer can make a call display your bank's name, an agency, or a local number. The number on your screen proves only what the caller chose to display. The bank fraud department script is the most polished version. The caller reports a suspicious transaction, then instructs you to move money to a safe account, share a code sent to your phone, or install an app. Every step is the theft.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A woman receives an evening call displaying her bank's name. The caller names a recent genuine purchase, then reports a second, large charge she did not make. He says the account is compromised and walks her through moving her balance to a protected account he provides. The receiving account belongs to the scammer's network. This could have stopped at the instruction to move money. The signal most people miss is the accurate detail at the start. One true purchase, easily obtained from stolen records, sold every false instruction that followed.

HANG-UP SCRIPT: Say: I do not handle account matters on incoming calls. I am hanging up and calling the number on my card. Then hang up and call the number on the back of your card or on a statement.

COMMON MISTAKE: Calling back the number that just called you and feeling reassured when the same operation answers professionally. Callback protection only works with a number from an independent source you already hold.

LESSON 2 — AGENCY, SUPPORT, AND FAMILY EMERGENCY CALLS
Other scripts borrow different authority. Tech support callers claim your computer reported a problem and ask you to install remote access software so they can fix it, then use that access to reach your accounts. Family emergency callers claim a relative is in trouble and needs money quietly and quickly. One rule covers all of them: no incoming call gets money, codes, personal information, or device access, whatever the story. Verify emergencies through people and numbers you already know, and agree on a family code word in advance for genuine emergencies.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A man receives a call from a technician claiming his computer has been sending error reports and is infected. The caller directs him to install a remote access tool and read out the connection code. Once connected, the caller shows supposed intrusions and begins opening the man's banking page. The man pulls the power cord and calls his bank from another phone. This could have stopped at the installation step. The signal most people miss is the direction of contact. Legitimate support responds when you contact it through official channels; it does not arrive uninvited by phone.

FAMILY CHECKLIST: Agree on a code word for genuine emergencies. Agree that secrecy demands on a call are always a scam sign.

COMMON MISTAKE: Staying on the call to gather information or outsmart the caller. Vishing callers are trained on hesitation, and every minute gives them material to use. Hanging up early is the skill.

HANDS-ON EXERCISE
A caller displaying your bank's name says your account is being drained, and a code has been sent to your phone which you must read aloud now to stop it. Decision point one: the caller ID and an accurate account detail make the call feel verified. The correct path is to treat neither as proof. Decision point two: the code arrives by text during the call, seeming to confirm the story. The correct path is to refuse to read it. A code triggered during a stranger's call is a weapon aimed at your account, not a rescue tool. Decision point three: the caller warns that hanging up will complete the theft. The correct path is to hang up and call the number on your card.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Vishing is fraud by voice, and spoofed caller ID makes the display meaningless.
- Your bank will never ask you to move money, share a code, or install an app on a call.
- Hang up and call back on the number from your card or statement. Every time.
- No incoming call receives money, codes, personal data, or device access.

GLOSSARY
Vishing — Phishing conducted over voice phone calls
Spoofed Caller ID — A faked display name or number chosen by the caller
Remote Access Tool — Software allowing another person to view and control your device
Family Code Word — A private word agreed in advance to confirm a genuine family emergency
Impostor Scam — Fraud where the scammer pretends to be a trusted agency, company, or person

MODULE OVERVIEW
Level: Foundations | Time: ~15 minutes
Prerequisites: none

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
After this module you can:
- Explain why gift cards are never a legitimate way to pay a debt, fine, or support bill
- Recognize the pressure scripts used to keep you on the phone and moving
- Apply a stop-and-verify routine before buying a gift card at someone else's direction
- Describe what to do in the first minutes after sharing gift card numbers

LESSON 1 — WHY GIFT CARDS ARE THE SCAMMER'S FAVORITE PAYMENT
A gift card is cash in a different wrapper. Once you read the numbers off the back to someone, the money is gone. That is why scammers ask for gift cards rather than a check or card payment. A legitimate business, agency, utility, or tech support company will never ask you to pay with gift cards. Not for taxes, bail, a repair, or a bill. If the payment method is a gift card, the request is a scam, every time. Scammers also keep you on the phone while you buy the cards, so no cashier, relative, or second thought can interrupt the script.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A woman receives a call claiming her computer triggered illegal activity and her account will close unless she pays a fee at once. She is told to buy gift cards and to say they are family gifts if asked. She reads the numbers while still on the phone, and the balance is drained in minutes. This could have stopped at the payment request itself: no support company charges fees payable only in gift cards. The signal most people miss is the instruction to lie to the cashier. A legitimate payment never requires a cover story.

WHAT TO DO INSTEAD: Hang up. If you fear the problem is real, contact the company yourself using a number from its official website or a bill you already have, never a number the caller gave you. In a store, tell the cashier the truth about why you are buying the cards. Ask for their help.

COMMON MISTAKE: Believing the story because the caller knew personal details. Knowing your name or address proves nothing. Judge the request, not the details around it.

LESSON 2 — SPOTTING THE SCRIPT AND BREAKING IT
These scams use a small set of scripts. A government impostor threatens arrest or loss of benefits. A tech support impostor claims your device is compromised. A utility impostor threatens to cut power within the hour. A family emergency impostor claims a loved one is in jail or hospital and needs gift cards for fees. The stories differ; the structure is identical: urgency, secrecy, isolation, and gift cards. Break the structure rather than debating the story.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A man is told his grandson was arrested after an accident and needs gift cards for a court fee before release. A second voice, young and distressed, calls him Grandpa. Driving toward a store, he calls his daughter on another phone without ending the first call. She confirms his grandson is at school. This could have stopped at the secrecy instruction, when the caller said telling anyone would delay the release. The signal most people miss is simple: real courts, jails, and hospitals do not take gift cards and do not forbid you from calling family.

WHAT TO SAY: I do not pay anyone with gift cards. I am hanging up and calling back on a number I trust. Then hang up. Do not stay on the line to be polite or ask questions. Afterwards, verify with family or the agency, report the attempt.

COMMON MISTAKE: Paying a recovery fee after a loss. No legitimate service charges a fee, especially in gift cards, to recover gift card funds. A second payment only increases the loss.

HANDS-ON EXERCISE
A caller says your electricity will be shut off in 45 minutes unless you pay an overdue balance with gift cards. Decision point one: the caller demands you stay on the phone while you drive to a store. The correct path is to hang up; staying on the line removes your chance to think and verify. Decision point two: you worry the bill might truly be overdue. The correct path is to call your utility on the number from your own bill and ask about your account. That call, not the incoming one, tells you the truth. Decision point three: a neighbor says she paid quickly in the same situation and her power stayed on. The correct path is still not to pay. Her power stayed on because it was never going to be cut.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- If someone demands payment in gift cards, it is a scam. No exceptions.
- Urgency, secrecy, and staying on the phone are control tactics.
- Never use a cover story at the register. Tell the cashier the truth.
- Verify any alarming claim yourself, on a number you already trust.
- After a loss, contact the card issuer, keep the receipt, and report it.

GLOSSARY
Gift Card Drain — Emptying a card balance right after the victim shares its numbers
Impostor Scam — Fraud where the scammer pretends to be a trusted agency or person
Isolation Tactic — Keeping a victim on the phone and secretive so others cannot intervene
Cover Story — A false explanation a scammer gives a victim to use at the register
Recovery Scam — A second fraud promising to recover lost money for an upfront fee

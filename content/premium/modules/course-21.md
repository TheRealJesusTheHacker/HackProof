MODULE OVERVIEW
Level: Intermediate | Time: ~22 minutes
Prerequisites: none

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
After this module you can:
- Describe the full pig butchering funnel from first text to total loss
- Spot the signals that a friendship or romance is being steered to investing
- Explain why a small allowed withdrawal does not prove a platform is real
- Refuse the deposit and withdrawal-fee trap with a clear exit plan
- Document and report the scam in a way investigators can use

LESSON 1 — THE FUNNEL: WRONG NUMBER TO FAKE FORTUNE
Pig butchering is a long con: fattening a victim with attention and fake profits before taking everything. It rarely starts with a pitch. It starts with a friendly wrong-number text, dating match, or social message that seems like a mistake: Sorry, is this Kevin? You reply that it is not, the sender jokes, and keeps talking. Over weeks, the chat becomes friendship or romance. Photos and daily check-ins build trust.

Only later does money enter, casually. Your friend mentions an uncle or mentor with inside knowledge of crypto trading, and shows screenshots of gains. You try a small amount on a professional-looking platform. Your balance rises. You may even withdraw a small amount. That withdrawal is staged: it costs the scammer little and convinces you to deposit far more.

CASE BREAKDOWN: Denise answers a wrong-number text and chats daily for a month with a man who says he works overseas. He teaches her to buy crypto on a legitimate exchange, then move it to his trading site. Her $1,000 becomes $1,400 on screen. She withdraws $200. Reassured, she deposits $28,000. The balance shows $51,000. Where it could have stopped: before the first transfer to his platform. No legitimate mentor routes a new friend to an unfamiliar site. The signal most people miss: the relationship and the investment are one operation, designed to get the big deposit. Once it lands, the tone changes to pressure and deadlines.

COMMON MISTAKE: Judging a platform by its design and balance. Both are scammer-controlled. A balance is just numbers until money is back in your bank.

LESSON 2 — THE WITHDRAWAL TRAP AND HOW TO GET OUT
The endgame begins when you try to withdraw. Suddenly there is a tax, verification fee, or hold you must pay first. You are told paying will release everything. It will not. Each payment produces a new fee. Money sent by wire or crypto is effectively irreversible, so every extra payment deepens the loss.

Protect yourself earlier. Treat any unsolicited contact that drifts toward investing as a stop sign. Never invest on advice from someone met only online, never move money to a platform they chose, and never let anyone coach deposits by chat or screen share.

If you are already in, stop sending money today. Save everything: chats, profiles, wallet addresses, transaction IDs, platform names, web addresses, and bank records. Report to the FTC and FBI IC3, notify your bank and exchange, and tell a trusted person. Shame keeps this scam profitable. Victims are often careful people; this scam works over weeks precisely because a quick pitch would fail.

COMMON MISTAKE: Paying one last fee because you have already invested so much. Past deposits are gone unless law enforcement recovers them. A fee payment does not increase your chance of withdrawal.

HANDS-ON EXERCISE
For three weeks you have chatted with someone from a dating app who is kind, attentive, and wealthy. She suggests you try the crypto platform her uncle analyzes, starting with $500.
Decision point 1: The site looks professional and your $500 shows as $620. Do you add more. Correct path: No. On-screen gains prove nothing. The platform was supplied by the person who benefits from your deposit.
Decision point 2: You withdraw $100 successfully and she says, See, it is safe, now do the big trade before Friday. Correct path: Still no. A small withdrawal is a known trust-building step before the large deposit.
Decision point 3: After a $10,000 deposit, support demands a $2,500 tax fee to release funds. Correct path: Pay nothing. Preserve chats and transaction records, contact your bank and exchange, and report to the FTC and IC3.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Pig butchering combines romance or friendship with a fake investment platform
- Rising balances and a small withdrawal are staged to earn a big deposit
- The withdrawal fee or tax demand is the final trap, not a real release step
- Never invest on the advice of someone you have only met online
- Stop paying, preserve all records, and report to the FTC and IC3

GLOSSARY
Pig butchering — A long con that builds trust and fake profits before taking a large sum
Funnel — The staged path from first contact to deposit to total loss
Fake platform — A scammer-controlled site that displays invented balances and trades
Wallet address — The destination string used to send crypto to another holder
Staged withdrawal — A small real payout designed to make a fake platform seem legitimate
Exit trap — Invented fees or taxes demanded before a withdrawal that never comes

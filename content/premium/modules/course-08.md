MODULE OVERVIEW
Level: Foundations | Time: ~18 minutes
Prerequisites: none

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
After this module you can:
- Recognize fake payment confirmations and overpayment check schemes
- Verify a payment inside your own account instead of trusting a screenshot or email
- Protect yourself when meeting a buyer or seller in person
- Explain why you should never refund a difference or ship before funds clear

LESSON 1 — PAYMENTS THAT LOOK REAL AND ARE NOT
Marketplace scams succeed because the proof of payment looks convincing. A buyer sends a screenshot showing a completed transfer, an email appearing to come from a payment service, or a check for more than your asking price. None of that is payment. Screenshots can be edited, emails fabricated, and checks counterfeited. Your only reliable evidence is money that has cleared inside your own account, checked by you. The overpayment scheme adds a story: the buyer claims the extra was a mistake and asks you to return the difference or pay a shipper.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A seller lists a table. A buyer agrees to the price, says he is out of town, and mails a check for far more, explaining the extra covers his movers, whom the seller should pay in cash on arrival. She deposits the check, sees the amount appear, and pays men who arrive claiming to be the movers. The bank then reverses the deposit: the check is counterfeit. This could have stopped at the overpayment itself. Extra money plus a request to pay a third party is the scheme. The signal most people miss is the provisional deposit. Funds appearing is not funds cleared, and a counterfeit check can take days to discover.

SAFE PAYMENT CHECKLIST: Check payment inside your own account, never from a screenshot or buyer email. Wait for funds to clear, not merely appear. Refuse any payment above the agreed price. Never pay movers or fees out of a buyer's payment.

COMMON MISTAKE: Shipping because a confirmation email says payment is held until you provide a tracking number. That email exists to get the item shipped before you discover there was never a payment.

LESSON 2 — SAFE MEETINGS AND CLEAN CLOSINGS
Buyers face mirror-image risks. Sellers may demand deposits to hold popular items, send links to fake payment pages, or advertise items they never intend to ship. A deposit sent to hold a bargain is usually the whole theft. Keep communication and payment inside the platform where possible. For local sales, meet in a public place, ideally one designated for exchanges, such as a police station parking area. Accept cash or a payment you watch clear in your own app at the meeting. Walk away if terms change.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A buyer finds a game console listed well below typical resale prices. The seller asks for a deposit by payment app to hold it until evening. The buyer sends it. The listing disappears and the account stops replying. This could have stopped at the deposit request. Urgency plus an off-platform deposit to a stranger is not a hold; it is the product being sold. The signal most people miss is the price itself. A price far below the going rate is not luck. It is bait chosen to make caution feel expensive.

WHAT TO SAY: I pay the agreed price, in the agreed way, when the item is in front of me, and not before. A legitimate seller can accept those terms or sell to someone else.

COMMON MISTAKE: Assuming a friendly profile with history proves a counterparty is safe. Accounts are bought, borrowed, and hijacked. Profile warmth is not verification. Terms and cleared funds are what protect you.

HANDS-ON EXERCISE
You list a phone and a buyer offers full price at once, without questions, if you ship today. Decision point one: the buyer sends an email that looks like a payment confirmation, saying funds release once you enter a tracking number. The correct path is to check your own account by opening the payment app yourself. There is no payment. Decision point two: the buyer offers extra money for fast shipping. The correct path is to refuse. Added money for added haste is pressure, not generosity. Decision point three: the buyer says you are insulting his honesty. The correct path is to end the sale calmly. Escalating emotion is a tool to make you ship an unpaid item.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Screenshots, emails, and provisional deposits are not payment. Cleared funds are.
- Overpayment is never an accident to fix. It is the scheme itself.
- Never ship, refund a difference, or pay a third party before funds clear.
- Meet in public, keep agreed terms, and test items before paying.

GLOSSARY
Overpayment Scam — A fake payment above the price, with a request to return the difference
Counterfeit Check — A fake check that may appear deposited before a bank finds it invalid
Cleared Funds — Money fully settled in your account, no longer subject to deposit reversal
Provisional Deposit — A temporary credit shown before a check is verified
Off-Platform Payment — Payment moved outside the marketplace, losing its protections

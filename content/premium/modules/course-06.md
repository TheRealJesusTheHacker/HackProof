MODULE OVERVIEW
Level: Intermediate | Time: ~20 minutes
Prerequisites: none

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
After this module you can:
- Explain why cryptocurrency payments are effectively irreversible once sent
- Identify guaranteed-return, double-your-money, and celebrity-endorsement lures
- Recognize long-con investment fraud and fake trading platforms
- Describe what to do, and what not to do, after sending crypto to a scammer

LESSON 1 — IRREVERSIBLE MONEY, IRRESISTIBLE PROMISES
Cryptocurrency moves without a bank in the middle, which removes the safety nets you rely on with a card or bank transfer. There is no chargeback and no fraud department that can pull a payment back because you were deceived. Once crypto reaches a wallet a scammer controls, it is usually gone. Scammers pair it with promises that should stop you cold: guaranteed returns do not exist in any real investment, and no promotion doubles your deposit.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A man sees a social media post using a well-known business figure's image, promising to double any crypto sent to a wallet during a giveaway, as spots run out. He sends a modest amount, then a larger amount when told the first fell below a qualifying minimum. Neither amount returns. This could have stopped at the promise itself. The signal most people miss is the manufactured minimum. Moving the threshold after you pay converts a cautious test into a major loss.

VERIFICATION CHECKLIST: Never invest based on a social post, message, or livestream alone. Reach any platform through its official address, not a link you were sent. Refuse any return described as guaranteed, and any fee required to withdraw your own balance.

COMMON MISTAKE: Treating a small successful withdrawal as proof a platform is real. Scammers often allow one small withdrawal to build confidence, then block everything after a large deposit. An early payout can be part of the setup.

LESSON 2 — FAKE PLATFORMS AND LONG-CON INVESTMENT FRAUD
The most damaging crypto scams are patient. In a scheme often called pig butchering, a scammer builds a friendly or romantic connection over weeks, gradually introduces investing, and guides the victim onto a fake trading platform. The platform shows impressive gains that are simply numbers on a screen. At withdrawal, the platform demands taxes, fees, or a security deposit first. Paying produces new demands, never a withdrawal. Fake support agents ask for your wallet recovery phrase; that phrase is total control, and no legitimate service will ever ask for it.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A woman is contacted by a man who claims he messaged the wrong number. He mentions he trades crypto with his uncle's guidance and offers to show her. She starts small on a platform he recommends, watches her balance climb, and withdraws a small amount successfully. She deposits a large sum. When she tries to withdraw it, the platform freezes her account and demands a tax payment in more crypto. This could have stopped at the first deposit, onto a platform a stranger recommended. The signal most people miss is the source of the advice. Guidance from a new online contact who also supplies the platform is a conflict that should end the conversation.

WHAT TO SAY AND DO: Tell any online contact who raises investing: I do not invest through platforms other people recommend in messages, and I do not discuss my finances here. Then stop engaging. If you already deposited, do not pay withdrawal fees.

COMMON MISTAKE: Borrowing money, using retirement funds, or keeping the investment secret because the contact advised privacy. Secrecy protects the scam.

HANDS-ON EXERCISE
A friendly contact you have known online for a month shows you a screenshot of his trading gains and offers to walk you through your first trade tonight. Decision point one: he sends a link to the platform and says to register before a market window closes. The correct path is to decline and not use the link. Decision point two: you say you want to research first, and he acts hurt, saying you do not trust him. The correct path is to hold your boundary. Emotional pressure is further evidence, not a reason to comply. Decision point three: you later see the same platform praised in a video comment thread. The correct path is still not to deposit. Scammers seed fake praise, and comments are not regulation or proof of withdrawals.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Crypto payments are irreversible. There is no chargeback to save you.
- Guaranteed returns and double-your-money offers are always scams.
- Never share your wallet recovery phrase with anyone, for any reason.
- Investment advice plus a supplied platform from an online contact is a stop signal.

GLOSSARY
Cryptocurrency — Digital assets transferred on a network without a bank intermediary
Wallet — Software or a device holding the keys that control your crypto
Recovery Phrase — A word list restoring full control of a wallet
Pig Butchering — Long-con fraud building trust before steering a victim into fake investing
Fake Exchange — A counterfeit trading site showing invented balances to lure deposits

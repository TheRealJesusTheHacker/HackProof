MODULE OVERVIEW
Level: Intermediate | Time: ~25 minutes
Prerequisites: AI & Deepfake Scams, Account Takeover Defense, SIM Swap Defense

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
After this module you can:
- Apply one verification habit to any request, whatever channel it arrives by
- Use the 24-hour rule to remove urgency from money decisions
- Build a written personal verification checklist for your accounts and family
- Review and refresh your defenses on a recurring schedule

LESSON 1 — THE HABIT: VERIFY INDEPENDENTLY, EVERY TIME
This course reduces to one habit: when a request asks for money, credentials, codes, or secrecy, verify it independently before you act. Independently means through a channel you choose and already trust, not the channel that brought the request. A text is verified by a call to a saved number, an email in the official app, a voice by a callback and a code word. The channel changes. The habit does not.

This works against scams you have never seen, because it depends on noticing the shape of the request, not recognizing the trick. Ask three questions of anything urgent. Who is really asking, and how do I know? What is being asked for: money, a code, a login, secrecy? What happens if I wait and check first? A legitimate request survives the wait.

Build the pause into your hands, not just your intentions. Put the phone face down and make the verification call from your saved contacts, not from the message in front of you. Never use a link, number, or reply button supplied by the request itself.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A homeowner gets a text that her power will be cut within the hour unless she pays through a link. The message uses her utility's name and her street. She opens the utility's app instead, sees a normal balance, and calls the number on her bill. No cut is scheduled. Personal details made a mass message feel targeted, and she stopped it by choosing her own channel. The signal most people miss: feeling targeted is not evidence. Realism is not verification.

COMMON MISTAKE: Verifying inside the same thread, by replying "is this really you?" Whoever sent it will say yes. Verification only counts when it leaves that channel.

LESSON 2 — THE 24-HOUR RULE AND YOUR PERSONAL CHECKLIST
Urgency is the scammer's main tool, so take it off the table in advance. Adopt the 24-hour rule: any new, unexpected request to send money, buy gift cards, or change payment details waits 24 hours, however convincing the reason for haste. Genuine bills can be confirmed and still paid within a day. A request that cannot survive a day of checking is telling you what it is. Tell your family this rule exists, so invoking it needs no apology.

Now make your system written. Build your checklist in four parts. One: verification contacts, saved numbers for family, your bank, and your carrier. Two: your code word and the rule that no money moves from a single call. Three: your defenses: app-based authentication, unique passwords, a carrier PIN, and port-out protection. Four: your first-hour plan: isolate, secure email, call the bank, freeze credit, report, document.

Keep the checklist where you will find it under stress, and review it every few months.

CASE BREAKDOWN: A retiree receives a polished email saying his investment account needs immediate action through a provided link. His checklist says account problems are checked only through the app and the number on his statement. He follows it and learns his account is fine. A decision made calmly overruled one that pressure tried to force. The signal most people miss: willpower is unreliable under pressure. A written system is not.

COMMON MISTAKE: Building a checklist once and never revisiting it. A stale checklist fails quietly.

HANDS-ON EXERCISE
Situation: A message from your manager's real email address asks you to buy gift cards today for a client emergency, keep it quiet, and send photos of the codes. It feels genuine.

Decision point 1: The request is urgent and secret. Correct path: both are triggers. Your habit applies before you weigh how genuine it feels.
Decision point 2: You consider replying to ask if it is real. Correct path: do not verify in the thread. Call your manager through a known channel.
Decision point 3: Your manager knows nothing about it and a colleague got the same message. Correct path: warn your team through normal channels, report the message, and note the incident for your checklist review.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- One habit covers every scam: verify independently before money or codes move.
- Never verify using a link, number, or reply supplied by the request itself.
- The 24-hour rule removes urgency from money decisions.
- Write your checklist: contacts, code word, defenses, and first-hour plan.
- Review the checklist every few months and after any scare.

GLOSSARY
Verify independently — Checking a request through a channel you choose and trust.
Scam radar — The practiced habit of noticing request shapes that signal fraud.
24-hour rule — A standing delay before acting on any new, unexpected money request.
Code word — A private word agreed in advance to confirm a family member's identity.
Standing rule — A decision made in advance that applies in the moment.
